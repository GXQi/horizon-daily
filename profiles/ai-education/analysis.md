# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of timely AI-in-education news for readers who work in or follow edtech, teaching, school or university administration, and AI literacy policy.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Groundbreaking.** A concrete change with system-wide reach: a national or state AI curriculum or exam policy taking effect, a large institution or system deploying AI teaching at scale with reported results, a measured breakthrough in learning outcomes, or a shutdown/restriction that changes what millions of students can use.
- **7-8: High value.** A real deployment, procurement, or study with specifics: named institution or product, number of students, measured or reported effect, timeline, or cost. New AI-literacy standards, assessment-rule changes, and credible independent evaluations also belong here.
- **5-6: Interesting.** Product launches with limited evidence, single-course or single-department pilots, teacher-survey findings, and policy debates that have not yet produced a rule.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Opinion pieces with no new facts, listicles, vendor marketing, conference announcements, and general education news that only mentions AI in passing.
- **0-2: Noise.** Spam, unverified rumor, pure promotion, and education news with no AI content at all.

# Evaluation guidance

Prefer items with concrete actors, numbers, and effective dates over commentary. Treat claimed learning gains as vendor claims unless the source describes an independent or controlled evaluation. Weight policy and assessment rules highly because they change what teachers and students must do. Do not reward hype or stock AI-in-schools rhetoric. General edtech funding rounds matter only when the specific product is AI-based.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
